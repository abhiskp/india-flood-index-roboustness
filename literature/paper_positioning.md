# Paper Positioning

## Provisional Title

Evidence not yet sufficient. Working title: **Do Complex Flood-Priority Indices Improve District Rankings? A Leakage-Free Validation Study in India**

## Primary Research Question

Do complex composite flood-priority indices and predictive models provide more useful district rankings than simple, objective-specific historical indicators?

## Secondary Research Questions

- Can historical district flood records identify districts at elevated future recorded flood-fatality burden under strict non-overlapping temporal validation?
- Does separating physical hazard from exposure and recorded human consequences improve interpretability and external correspondence with satellite-observed inundation?
- Are apparent improvements robust to state-block uncertainty and simple baseline comparisons?

## Proposed Contribution

The proposed contribution is not another flood index. It is a validation and construct-separation study showing how published composite flood-priority rankings, satellite-observed physical inundation and future recorded human consequences answer different questions. The contribution may be strongest as a methodological note or focused research article if literature screening confirms that strict temporal holdout and simple-baseline comparisons are uncommon in flood-priority ranking studies.

## Comparison With Previous Studies

- DFSI provides the original national district flood severity index for India.
- Flood vulnerability index literature establishes many composite approaches but limited validation.
- Disaster-index validation studies show that observed outcomes can be used to test indices, and that index performance depends on the outcome.
- Composite-indicator methodology shows that weights and realized importance can diverge and that robustness analysis is necessary.
- Evidence not yet sufficient on whether previous flood-priority studies routinely test against simple historical baselines or later non-overlapping outcomes.

## Likely Reviewer Objections

- The study may be framed too broadly if it claims novelty for index validation in general.
- GFD is not complete and only partly overlaps the DFSI period.
- The future-fatality target uses only district-assignable deaths and excludes unallocated multi-district fatality totals.
- Fatality reporting drift may limit interpretation of temporal prediction.
- Past flood-event count performing as well as or better than the fitted model may weaken the case for a predictive-modelling paper.
- Boundary harmonisation uncertainty may affect district-level results.

## Which Result Is the Main Result

Current evidence points to the main result being methodological: complex indices and fitted models do not automatically provide better district rankings than objective-specific simple historical indicators. The clearest empirical result is that past flood-event frequency is a very strong baseline for future recorded fatalities and is not robustly beaten by the fitted model.

## Which Analyses Belong in Supplementary Material

- DFSI arithmetic debugging and reproduction mechanics.
- Detailed Monte Carlo weight-sensitivity outputs.
- Detailed cutoff-sensitivity tables.
- Full district-level satellite matching audit.
- In-sample exploratory Monte Carlo weight correlation analysis.

## Claims That Must Not Be Made

- Do not claim exact DFSI reproduction.
- Do not claim DFSI is invalid because satellite or temporal correlations are imperfect.
- Do not claim the fitted model is a superior national predictive ranking.
- Do not claim full-period DFSI predicts outcomes inside the same period.
- Do not claim changes in recorded flood events are physical trends without reporting caveats.
- Do not claim one universal flood-priority ranking works equally well for physical inundation and future human consequences.

## Evidence Still Required

- Direct evidence on temporal validation practices in flood-priority rankings.
- Direct evidence on simple baseline comparisons in composite flood-index studies.
- More India-specific district flood-risk and flood-mortality studies.
- Literature on reporting bias and completeness in Indian disaster/flood records.
- Evidence on how journals frame validation studies: full article versus methodological note versus reproducibility/data note.

## Recommended Paper Type

Evidence not yet sufficient. Current direction: **methodological note or focused research article**, unless additional literature shows the temporal/simple-baseline contribution is already common.

## Abandon Criteria

Abandon or convert to an internal technical report if further literature reveals multiple close studies that already perform India district-scale flood-priority validation with satellite inundation, non-overlapping future fatality outcomes, simple historical baselines and geographic uncertainty checks.
## Full-Text Overlap Screen Decision

### Final Recommended Paper Question

Do complex flood-priority indices and fitted predictive models improve objective-specific India district rankings relative to simple historical indicators under independent satellite and leakage-free temporal validation?

### Final Contribution

A focused validation study showing that mixed composite flood-priority rankings should be tested against the specific outcome they are meant to support. In this project, physical-hazard measures better match satellite-observed inundation, while later recorded fatality burden is not robustly better predicted by a fitted model than by simple past flood-event frequency.

### Strongest Competing Paper

Tellman et al. (2020), because it validates flood social-vulnerability indicators against observed flood deaths and property damage and compares model specifications including SoVI and flood-intensity-only models.

### Exact Difference From That Paper

Tellman et al. (2020) is a US county/event social-vulnerability validation study using same-period flood deaths and property damage. Our study is an India district flood-priority validation centred on DFSI, adds independent satellite-observed physical inundation, uses strict non-overlapping temporal validation for later recorded fatalities, compares against simple district historical baselines and applies state-block uncertainty checks.

### Suitable Article Type

Short methodological article.

### Whether Satellite and Temporal Analyses Belong In One Paper

Yes, if framed as construct separation: satellite inundation validates physical flood burden, while future fatalities validate a human-impact prioritisation objective. They should not be merged into one universal target.

### Supplementary Analyses

- DFSI arithmetic reproduction/debugging details.
- Full Monte Carlo and cutoff sensitivity tables.
- Full GFD boundary matching audits.
- Full district-level predictions and unmatched/ambiguous records.
- In-sample exploratory weight optimisation.

### Final Working Title

**Do Complex Flood-Priority Rankings Add Value? Satellite and Temporal Validation of District Flood Prioritisation in India**
