# Project Findings for Literature Review

This summary is derived only from the completed project reports and should be used as the internal evidence boundary during literature screening.

## Final Defensible Claims

- Physical inundation should be evaluated separately from exposure and recorded human consequences.
- Satellite-observed physical inundation corresponded more strongly with physical-hazard-only measures than with the mixed six-component DFSI.
- DFSI weight changes left national rank correlations high but altered district priority membership.
- Historical flood records can be tested prospectively only with strict non-overlapping windows.
- A leakage-free future-fatality ranking is feasible only as a narrow claim: recorded fatalities in 2005-2023 predicted from 1986-2004 district flood history.
- The fitted future-fatality model showed predictive signal, but did not robustly outperform simple past flood-event frequency.
- Reporting drift and sparse fatality outcomes limit strong predictive claims.

## Rejected Claims

- Full-period DFSI should not be described as predicting outcomes inside the same period.
- Reconstructed full-period DFSI should not be used as a temporal predictor for subperiod outcomes.
- Full-period flooded-area or GFD summaries should not be used as temporal predictors for overlapping outcome windows.
- Multi-district event fatalities should not be duplicated across districts.
- The project should not claim to have created a universally better national flood-severity ranking.
- Weak or moderate satellite correlation should not be interpreted as invalidating DFSI.

## Major Limitations

- GFD covers 2000-2018 and is not a complete inventory of every Indian flood.
- GFD measures physical inundation, whereas DFSI combines hazard, exposure and recorded human consequences.
- Boundary vintage mismatch and unresolved district matches remain.
- Fatality reporting is incomplete and changes across periods.
- Multi-district fatality totals are not allocated to individual districts, making district fatality burden conservative.
- Many districts have zero recorded future fatalities, creating ties and sparse outcomes.
- Population is only a secondary sensitivity predictor because its reference year is not documented.
- State-block validation weakens claims of model superiority.

## Recommended Tables and Figures

- Predictive temporal feasibility and final ranking tables.
- Final model and benchmark comparison.
- Satellite physical-hazard validation as construct-validity support.
- Reporting completeness figure.
- Detailed DFSI arithmetic debugging should remain out of the manuscript.
- Monte Carlo DFSI sensitivity can be supplementary unless central to framing.
- In-sample weight optimisation should be excluded from causal or predictive claims.

## Final Suggested Research Question

Can historical district flood records identify districts at elevated future recorded flood-fatality burden, and how does this compare with separating physical hazard from exposure and consequences?

## Current Publication Direction

The completed quantitative work supports **GO WITH A NARROW CLAIM**, not a broad claim that complex indices or fitted models are generally superior to simple historical indicators.
